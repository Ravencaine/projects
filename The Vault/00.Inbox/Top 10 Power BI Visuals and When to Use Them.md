## A practical guide to choosing the right chart for the right insight

## Introduction

One of the biggest strengths of Power BI lies in its wide range of visuals. But choosing the right visual isn’t just about looks — it’s about telling the story your data needs to tell. The wrong choice can confuse your audience, while the right one makes insights crystal clear.

Here’s a rundown of the Top 10 Power BI visuals and when to use them effectively.

## 1\. Clustered Column Chart

📊 Best for comparing values across categories.

- Example: Sales by region, revenue by product category.
- Use when you want to show differences between groups.

## 2\. Stacked Column Chart

📊 Best for showing parts of a whole, broken down by categories.

- Example: Total sales split by product type per quarter.
- Use when you want to show contribution of subcategories.

## 3\. Line Chart

📈 Best for showing trends over time.

- Example: Monthly website traffic, sales growth across years.
- Use when time is the key dimension.

## 4\. Area Chart

📈 Best for showing cumulative trends with volume.

- Example: Market share growth, cumulative sales.
- Use when you want to emphasize magnitude and progression.

## 5\. Pie & Donut Chart

⭕ Best for showing percentage contribution.

- Example: Market share by company, expense categories in a budget.
- Use when you want to show proportions — but keep categories under 5–6 for clarity.

## 6\. Table & Matrix

📋 Best for detailed, exact numbers.

- Example: Invoice-level data, performance breakdown by salesperson.
- Use when precise values matter more than visuals.

## 7\. Card Visuals

💳 Best for showing KPIs or single-number highlights.

- Example: Total sales, total customers, profit margin.
- Use when you want to display a key metric clearly.

## 8\. Gauge Visual

🎯 Best for progress toward a target.

- Example: Sales target vs achieved, project completion status.
- Use when you want to track performance against goals.

## 9\. Map Visuals (Filled & Bubble Maps)

🗺️ Best for geographic insights.

- Example: Sales by country, customers by city.
- Use when location is a key factor in your analysis.

## 10\. Scatter Chart

⚪ Best for finding relationships and outliers.

- Example: Revenue vs marketing spend, customer age vs purchase amount.
- Use when analyzing correlations between two variables.
