# Product Portfolio Pareto Principle in Retail

A small number of products typically drive a disproportionate share of total revenue. Identifying and prioritizing these products is a core retail strategy principle.

## Definition

The Pareto Principle (80/20 rule) states that roughly 80% of effects come from 20% of causes. In retail, this often manifests as a small fraction of SKUs generating the majority of revenue or profit.

## Key Points

- In most retail datasets, 10–20% of products account for 60–80% of total sales.
- Top-performing products should receive priority in inventory planning, supplier negotiations, and promotional budgets.
- The long tail of low-selling products consumes storage and management overhead relative to their financial contribution.
- Portfolio analysis surfaces which products are "strategic" vs. "background noise."
- The principle applies to customers and regions as well as products.

## Examples

| Product Group | # of SKUs | % of Catalog | % of Revenue |
|---------------|-----------|-------------|--------------|
| Top 20% performers | 50 | 20% | 72% |
| Bottom 80% performers | 200 | 80% | 28% |
