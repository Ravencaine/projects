# AI Can Appear Accurate While Being Wrong

AI-generated formulas, summaries, and charts can look polished and plausible while being factually incorrect, incomplete, or built on flawed logic. Human validation is always required.

## Expected Behaviour

A formula generated by Copilot or a DAX/M expression suggested by an AI tool should be correct — it was trained on millions of examples and the syntax looks right. You accept it as accurate.

## Actual Behaviour

AI can produce:
- Formulas with correct syntax but wrong logic (e.g., summing the wrong range, applying criteria to the wrong column)
- Summaries that omit key data points or misrepresent relationships
- Charts that are technically valid but misleading in scale, colour encoding, or data selection
- Answers that sound confident and detailed but are factually wrong (hallucination)

## Why It Happens

AI generates outputs probabilistically — it produces the most likely continuation of a prompt, not a verified correct answer. Syntax correctness does not imply logical correctness. In project management contexts, this is compounded by AI's lack of domain knowledge about specific projects, teams, or business rules.

## How to Handle It

1. **Always validate against known data points**: pick 3-5 rows and verify the AI output matches manually
2. **Check formula ranges**: confirm that referenced ranges cover the intended data and nothing more
3. **Cross-check summaries**: ask AI to summarize the same data twice and compare results — inconsistencies reveal errors
4. **Use AI for drafting, not decision-making**: AI suggests; humans approve. Never use AI output directly in a live project report without review.
5. **Set a sanity threshold**: for critical metrics (budget totals, schedule dates), calculate the expected result manually before accepting the AI output
