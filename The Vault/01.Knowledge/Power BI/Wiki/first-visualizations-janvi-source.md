## Summary

Beginner's guide to creating effective Power BI visualisations. Core principle: great visualisations answer one specific question, can be understood in under 5 seconds, and lead to a clear insight or action. Covers nine chart categories from basic comparison to advanced AI-powered visuals, with a chart selection decision tree and the seven most common beginner mistakes.

## The Foundational Rule

> **Great visualisations are not about showing ALL your data. They're about showing the RIGHT data in the CLEAREST possible way.**

Three questions every visualisation should answer:
1. **What specific question does this answer?**
2. **Can it be understood in 5 seconds or less?**
3. **Does it lead to a clear insight or action?**

## Chart Categories Covered

| Category | Chart types | Use case |
|----------|------------|----------|
| Comparison | Bar, Column, Clustered, Stacked | Comparing values across categories |
| Trend | Line, Area, Combo | Showing change over time |
| Part contribution | Pie, Donut, Treemap | Showing proportion of a whole |
| Single value | Card, KPI, Gauge | Displaying a single metric |
| Geographic | Map, Filled Map | Spatial distribution |
| Data display | Table, Matrix | Detailed structured data |
| Advanced analysis | Waterfall, Funnel, Scatter, AI visuals | Detailed or predictive analysis |

## Key Guidance Extracted

- Bar charts: for long category names or when horizontal comparison is clearer
- Column charts: for short names or time periods (time flows left to right)
- Line charts: for time-based trends only — not for categorical comparison
- Stacked vs clustered: stacked shows parts of a whole; clustered shows side-by-side comparison
- Always sort bar/column charts by value descending — highest first
- Pie charts: avoid with more than 5 categories; use bar chart instead

## Seven Common Visualisation Mistakes

1. Rainbow explosion (too many colours)
2. Using the wrong chart type for the data relationship
3. Showing all data when a filtered subset tells the story better
4. Cluttered axes and labels
5. Missing context (no baseline, target, or comparison period)
6. Inconsistent rounding across related metrics
7. Not sorting by the most meaningful dimension
