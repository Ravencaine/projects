— A Data Analyst’s Perspective from 20+ Years in the Field

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1400/format:webp/1*F4DOCfk9UHb6qSOFEfB3Tg.png)

When people imagine data analysis, they picture sleek dashboards, powerful machine learning models, and stories hidden in numbers. What they don’t picture is the hours spent elbow-deep in messy spreadsheets, duplicate entries, missing values, and typos that would make your head spin.

And let me tell you this, after working with data for over two decades: **cleaning data hurts more than building models.**

Let’s explore why.

**The Illusion of “Clean” Learning Data**

If you’ve taken any online data course, you’ve likely worked with a dataset like this:

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1100/format:webp/1*yb1C9yuWAFIy0l8YJnVhDA.png)

Looks nice, right? Every row has values. Columns are consistent. No random symbols. No missing fields.

That’s not data. That’s a **fantasy.**  
It’s the equivalent of practicing surgery on a plastic mannequin — you get the idea, but you’re not ready for the operating room.

**The Reality: Real-World Messy Data**

Now let me show you a slice of real-world student data I once encountered:

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1100/format:webp/1*CRJDf9jDFGPX-ZsM45_KRA.png)

Notice the chaos?

- Same student entered twice with different spellings.
- City names in five different formats.
- Missing values.
- Random “N/A” in text form.

Before you can even think about calculating averages or predicting outcomes, you’re forced to wrestle with this mess.

**Why Cleaning Hurts More Than Modeling**

1. **Time Sink**  
	Studies (and my own experience) suggest that **70–80% of a data analyst’s time is spent cleaning and preparing data**. Building the actual model? That’s often the easy 20%.
2. **Repetition Without Glory**  
	Modeling feels exciting — you’re testing hypotheses, building predictions, creating something new. Cleaning feels like chores. No one claps when you fix 200 misspelled city names.
3. **Human Judgment Required**  
	Algorithms can’t always guess if “NYC,” “New York,” and “N. York” should be treated the same. You, the human, have to decide. And sometimes, you have to negotiate with stakeholders to confirm what’s “correct.”
4. **It Never Ends**  
	Even after cleaning, new messy data keeps coming in. It’s not a one-time effort; it’s a maintenance job.

**How Much Time Does It Really Cost?**

Let’s put numbers on it:

- **Course dataset project**: You finish in 2–3 hours.
- **Real-world dataset project**: 2–3 days (or even weeks), with 70% of that time dedicated to data wrangling before you can build a single model.

In fact, the joke in the analytics community is:

*“The sexiest job of the 21st century is 80% janitorial work.”*

**Why It’s Still Worth It**

Here’s the twist: while painful, cleaning is where you **truly understand the data.**

When you go through inconsistencies, duplicates, and missing values, you’re not just fixing errors — you’re learning the story behind the system that generated the data. That story often reveals more insights than the model itself.

For example, in the student dataset above, duplicate names weren’t just a clerical error — they pointed to a broken enrolment process. Fixing the data helped improve business operations, not just reporting.

**Final Thoughts**

So, does cleaning data hurt more than building models? Absolutely.

But if you ask me after 20+ years, it’s in those messy, painful, frustrating hours that the *real value of a data analyst shines.* Anyone can run a regression on a clean dataset. But turning a swamp of messy records into something meaningful — that’s the art, craft, and grind of true analytics.

So next time you’re knee-deep in duplicates, missing values, and inconsistent formats, remember: you’re not just cleaning data — you’re creating the foundation on which every insight and decision will stand.

And yes, it hurts. But it’s the kind of hurt that makes you better.
