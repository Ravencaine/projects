*I built my Power BI dashboard and it helped me find the stories hidden in sales data.*

— — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — —

When I first looked at the Sales\_September\_2019 dataset I was not impressed.

It looked like a spreadsheet with rows and columns.

There were product names, prices order IDs purchase addresses and thousands of records.

At first it was data.

Nothing more.

After I put it into Microsoft Power BI I started to look at it differently.

Every row was a customer purchase.

Every product sold was important to a business.

Every number had a story waiting to be found.

That was the moment I realized that Business Analytics is not about looking at data it is about understanding what the data is trying to tell us.

— — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — —

**Why I Choose This Dataset?**

I was taking a Business Analytics Tools course. I wanted to work with a real dataset instead of a small sample.

The Sales\_September\_2019 dataset has over 11,000 sales records, which made it perfect for practicing data analysis and creating a dashboard.

I did not just want to make charts I wanted my dashboard to answer questions that every business wants to know.

Which products are selling the most?

Which products make the money?

How can sales data help businesses make decisions?

I wanted my dashboard to answer these questions in a way.

— — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — —

**Preparing the Data**

Before I created my dashboard I prepared the dataset.

I checked for missing values, fixed incorrect data and removed unnecessary records to make sure the data was good and reliable.

At first I thought this step would be boring.

I wanted to start making charts away.

I soon realized that even the most attractive dashboard is useless if the data behind it is incorrect.

Preparing the data may not be visible in the dashboard but it is one of the most important steps in any analytics project.

A clean dataset always leads to insights.

— — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — —

**Building My First Power BI Dashboard**

After I prepared the data I started building my dashboard in Microsoft Power BI.

This was my part of the project.

Of looking at thousands of rows in Excel I was able to turn the information into interactive visualizations.

Each chart answered a business question.

In a few seconds I could understand how products were doing compare sales and find important trends.

That is what makes Power BI such a powerful business analytics tool.

It turns data into information that anyone can understand.

— — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — —

## Quantity Sold Analysis:-

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1400/format:webp/1*avpMDkJcGLGPCJlTvrcKIg.png)

The next visualization I made was about the quantity sold for each product.

At first I thought expensive products would be at the top of the chart.

I found that cheap products were bought much more often.

Customers may only buy a laptop once every years but they buy batteries charging cables and headphones whenever they need them.

This taught me that high customer demand is not always connected to product prices.

Understanding which products customers buy the most helps businesses plan their inventory and keep products.

— — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — —

**What I Learned:-**

Working on this project taught me a lot more than how to make charts.

It showed me how dashboards can make complex information simple and help businesses make decisions.

I also learned that every visualization should answer a business question instead of just showing data.

Importantly I understood that business analytics is not about collecting data.

It is about turning information into meaningful insights that people can easily understand and use.

— — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — — —

**Final Thoughts:-**

When I first downloaded this dataset I just saw a spreadsheet.

Today I see customer behavior, product demand, business performance and revenue opportunities.

This project completely changed the way I think about data.

Building my Power BI dashboard helped me understand how businesses use analytics to solve real problems and make better decisions.

This may be my Business Analytics project but it has motivated me to explore more real-world datasets and keep improving my Power BI skills.

Every dataset tells a story.

As future data analysts our job is to find that story.

I will keep working with Business Analytics and Power BI to discover stories in data.

Business Analytics and Power BI are tools, for anyone who wants to understand data and make better decisions.

I am excited to see what stories I can find in data using Business Analytics and Power BI.
