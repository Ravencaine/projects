# Demand ≠ Price: Cheap Products Often Sell More Frequently

High customer demand is not necessarily connected to product prices. Cheap consumable or accessory products (batteries, charging cables, headphones) are bought frequently because they are replaced often. Expensive products (laptops) are bought infrequently because they last longer. A sales dashboard that shows only revenue by product will miss this distinction.

## Key Points

- Products with low unit price can dominate quantity-sold metrics even if they contribute little to total revenue
- Customers buy cheap products whenever they need them; expensive products are planned, infrequent purchases
- Inventory planning should account for both quantity-sold patterns and revenue contribution — not just one metric
- A dashboard that shows only revenue rankings may hide which products drive customer touchpoints and repeat engagement

## Business Implication

Use both **quantity sold** and **revenue** as separate measures. Ranking products by quantity sold reveals fast-moving consumables. Ranking by revenue reveals high-value items. Neither ranking alone tells the full story.
