Three weeks with Claude Code swung it back open. hexaballs Mar 04, 2026 5 1 Share In the fall of 2014, I walked into a coding bootcamp at General Assembly in Hong Kong barely knowing what a terminal was. For three months, I lived inside that classroom. Front-end development, CSS, databases, Ruby on Rails, JavaScript, Git. I went from staring blankly at a command line to building a portfolio of websites and web apps. I remember the exact moment my brain shifted: I typed a few lines of code, hit enter, and something appeared on my screen. I could manipulate it. Shape it. Make it do things. A whole world of possibilities opened up. I started a freelance web design agency, and for 2 years built websites for small businesses and entrepreneurs around Hong Kong. It was scrappy and exciting. Eventually, things didn’t work out the way I hoped, but that experience brought me to a tech company, where I took a non-coding path entirely. I locked up my coding chops and that world of possibilities in a memory box and moved on. That was 11 years ago. Thanks for reading! Subscribe for free to receive new posts and support my work. Subscribe The memory box cracks open Three weeks ago, I attended an Obsidian and Claude Code workshop by Construct by Dee . And that memory box cracked wide open. I spent nearly every waking hour of my holiday trip to Tahoe setting up Claude Code inside my Obsidian second brain. Within days, I had built something I’d been trying to piece together for years: a personal AI assistant that actually works the way I think. I talk to it. I tell it what I did today, who I met, what I worked on. It files my notes, tags them to the right areas of my life, and structures everything in a system I designed. I told it I needed help deciding between a MacBook and a Mac Mini for my specific use cases. It set up a working session, ran the analysis, and presented me with decision frameworks. I didn’t write a single line of that logic myself. What I built in three weeks Health tracking on autopilot. Python scripts that automatically pull my sleep and recovery data from Whoop and my calorie and macro intake from MyFitnessPal into my daily notes. A mobile capture pipeline. A Telegram integration that lets me send articles and YouTube links from my phone, which Claude Code then processes into formatted notes with transcripts, summaries, and tags. Meeting notes that process themselves. A workflow that takes my Granola transcripts and extracts key takeaways, action items, and 1:1 highlights. Presentations in an hour. A workflow that synthesized three PowerPoint decks and meeting transcripts into 13 slides in about an hour. A task manager built for my brain. A fully custom task management system built exactly for how I work. I cancelled my Todoist subscription. None of this required me to be a developer. Claude wrote the code. I was the orchestrator. I knew what I wanted, gave it context, shaped the output, and iterated until it worked. The real lesson Over the past year, I’ve been experiencing what I call AI vertigo . New tools launching every other day. Agents, copilots, wrappers, platforms. The constant drumbeat of “this changes everything” followed by the quiet realization that I didn’t understand most of it. I felt lost. I felt FOMO. I felt a deep, nagging unease that I was falling behind and couldn’t catch up. Three weeks of building with Claude Code changed that completely. Not because I suddenly understood everything. But because I stopped trying to understand everything from the outside and started building from the inside. Working with the technology, hitting its limitations, figuring out workarounds, watching it fail and then watching it surprise me. I learned more about how AI actually works in three weeks of building than in a year of reading about it. The only way I’ve found to reduce the anxiety around the pace of change is to be deeply immersed in the application of it. Not watching from the sidelines. Not reading the newsletters. Building. For the first time since those Hong Kong days, I feel that same energy. That same sense of expansion. That same feeling that a whole world of possibilities just appeared in front of me. And the most important part? It’s genuinely fun! What’s the thing you’ve been wanting to build but haven’t started yet? What would it take to just open the terminal and try? Thanks for reading!
