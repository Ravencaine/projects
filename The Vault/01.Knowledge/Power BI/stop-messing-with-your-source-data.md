You put some data in, write a few formulas, sort the columns, and you're done. While that might work for simpler tasks, it's a recipe for disaster if your workbook grows. In this scenario, your data should sit in the background while you do the work elsewhere. Use Power Query as your data airlock Transform your figures without ever touching the original cells Close When you need to clean data— removing columns , filtering out nulls, or fixing date formats—don't start modifying the cells on the grid. If you do, you're permanently altering the source, and it takes a lot of effort to retrace your steps. Instead, make sure your data is structured as an Excel table , then load it to the Power Query Editor by clicking From Table/Range in the Data tab. This opens a separate environment where you can perform your modifications without touching the source. Then, when you're done, you click the top half of the Close & Load button in the Editor's Home tab to ping that freshly edited dataset back to a brand-new worksheet in your workbook. The magic of this "airlock" is that it doesn't just apply to the sheet you're currently in, either. Once you adopt the muscle memory of leaving the source data alone, you can apply it to raw datasets spread across multiple worksheets or even multiple files . The best part? You only have to do the work once. Because Power Query remembers the steps you took, you can simply click Data > Refresh whenever your source data changes. Excel will rerun your entire data-cleaning script, giving you a fresh output while the messy raw data stays safely in the background. The Get & Transform Data group in the Data tab is more than just a table loader—you can use it to pull in data from the web , external CSVs, or SQL databases. By connecting to these sources rather than copying and pasting them into your sheet, you ensure your master workbook remains lean, and your source data remains untouched. Create live "read-only" views with dynamic arrays Let formulas like FILTER and SORT do the heavy lifting Close Standard auto-filters are the quick-and-dirty way to look at data, but they're inherently disruptive to your workflow. They hide rows, change your view, and often lead to invisible data being accidentally missed during a copy-paste or calculation. Instead of toggling filters on your source, leave those cells as a continuous, predictable block of the whole picture, and use modern dynamic functions to display specific subsets. Functions like FILTER , SORTBY , and UNIQUE (available in modern versions of Excel) allow you to create live, reactive windows into your data. For example, instead of filtering a list to find "Pending" orders, you can write a single formula that pulls only those records. Because these are dynamic arrays, they spill into adjacent cells as needed . As long as you wrap your source data in an Excel table, your formulas are ready to grab any new or updated information, so you get a real-time view of your records without having to click a filter arrow or hide a single row in your master sheet. Let PivotTables and slicers handle the UI Create an interactive dashboard without changing a single source cell Close If you find yourself manually inserting "Total" or "Subtotal" rows directly into your dataset, you're setting yourself up for failure. These manual rows break the linear structure of your data and confuse Excel's sorting and filtering logic. Even if you toggle the Total Row feature built into structured tables, it handles hidden data in weird ways. Professional spreadsheets keep the math in a separate layer entirely. By using a PivotTable ( Insert > PivotTable ), you can summarize thousands of rows of data in seconds. PivotTables allow you to pull out high-level answers—like regional sales totals or average lead times—without modifying a single cell in the raw data. The best part of this approach is how it handles new information. Simply right-click the PivotTable and click Refresh to pull in the latest figures from your source ( some users may have auto-refresh depending on their Excel version). This ensures your summaries stay in sync with your source data without you having to rebuild your calculations every time a new row is added. To make this feel like a finished product, add slicers . These are visual buttons that act as the "user interface" for your table-formatted or PivotTable data.
