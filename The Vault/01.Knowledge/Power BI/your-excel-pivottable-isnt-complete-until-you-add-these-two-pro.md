Sign in to your How-To Geek account Many people treat a completed PivotTable as the finish line, but it's actually just the first step. In fact, pro-level Excel spreadsheets let users navigate the data primarily through Slicers and Timelines, minimizing the need to interact directly with the PivotTable grid. In minutes, you can turn static datasets into dynamic, app-like dashboards. Prepare your data: The foundation of a pro dashboard Start with a table for a future-proof PivotTable The end goal is to build a high-visibility control panel where clicking a button or scrubbing a timeline instantly updates your data. However, for those "remote controls" to work reliably, your data needs a solid architectural foundation . If you jump straight into a PivotTable with a static range of cells, it'll be blind to any new information you add later. Instead, format your source data as a dynamic table : Select your data and click Table in the Insert tab, or press Ctrl+T . Check My table has headers . Make sure each column in your table is correctly formatted . For example, the Date column must be in a recognized Date format, the Revenue column must be in Accounting or Currency , and so on. This ensures the PivotTable knows what type of data it's dealing with—which is crucial for later steps. With the table selected, open the Table Design tab and rename the table (such as T_Sales ) in the Table Name field. Close You're now ready to create your PivotTable: With any cell in your table selected, click the top half of the split PivotTable button in the Insert tab. In the PivotTable from table or range dialog, check New Worksheet , and click OK . In the PivotTable Fields pane , drag fields you want to use as row labels into the Rows area and your currency data into the Values area. Leave the Date field for now—we'll handle this later with the Timeline. As you do this, your PivotTable will take shape. Close This is the point where many people stop, because the PivotTable shows a neat summary of their data. However, they're missing two key visualizations: a Slicer and a Timeline. Slicers: The high-visibility remote control Stop hiding your essential filters in tiny menus Slicers are essentially visual buttons for your data. While they can be added to regular Excel tables as a visual filter, they reach their full potential in PivotTables—especially when connected to multiple PivotTables—acting as a central command hub for controlling your dashboard. Without them, users have to dig through clunky drop-down menus, overlooking key insights. Here's what to do: Create a second PivotTable from the same source table . For example, if your first PivotTable shows Revenue by Category, the second one might show Revenue by Salesperson. Name each PivotTable in the PivotTable Name field of the PivotTable Analyze tab to make linking Slicers across multiple PivotTables easier. Click anywhere inside one of the PivotTables, and in the PivotTable Analyze tab, click Insert Slicer . Select the field you want this Slicer to control, and click OK . Right-click the new Slicer and click Report Connections . In the dialog, check the boxes for every PivotTable you want this Slicer to control. Repeat this process for any additional fields you want to control with their own slicers. You could select multiple fields when inserting a Slicer, but doing so can be overwhelming on complex dashboards . Creating one Slicer at a time lets you link it immediately to the PivotTables you want, keeping track of connections and ensuring nothing is forgotten. Close Now, simply click a button in a Slicer to filter all connected PivotTables. For example, selecting Hardware in the Category Slicer updates both PivotTables to show only that field, and the second Slicer (Salesperson) shows which people are involved in hardware sales. To select multiple items within a Slicer, hold Ctrl while clicking, or click the Multi-Select icon (three checkmarks) in the Slicer header. To clear a Slicer, click the Clear Filter button (funnel with an X) in the top-right corner. The Timeline: Navigating dates with precision Scrub through time like a professional video editor If your data includes dates, your PivotTable simply isn't complete without a Timeline filter . Unlike standard filters, Timelines understand chronological hierarchy. You can't easily "drag" through a vertical drop-down list of 365 individual dates, but a Timeline lets you visually scrub through months, quarters, and years, turning your static list into a fluid, interactive experience that feels more like a modern web app
