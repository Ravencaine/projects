# Skills Being Automated vs. Skills Becoming More Valuable

A structured comparison of analyst tasks being displaced by AI vs. the skills that increase in value as AI handles routine work.

## Summary

AI is automating the tasks it can do well (repetitive, pattern-based, query-driven). The skills that remain irreplaceable — and grow more valuable — are those requiring judgment, context, and strategic thinking.

## Being Automated

### Manual Data Cleaning

- 60–70% of routine cleaning tasks can be automated via AI-assisted profiling
- Power Query AI suggestions handle many transform steps
- Still requires oversight for edge cases and domain-specific logic

### Creating Standard Visualisations

- Q&A Visual generates charts on demand from natural language
- Copilot creates standard visuals from plain-English requests
- Custom, non-standard visuals still require manual design

### Running Standard Reports

- Scheduled reports can be replaced by Smart Narratives + anomaly alerts
- On-demand Q&A replaces static weekly exports
- Ad-hoc requests partially handled by self-service Q&A

### Answering Repetitive Business Questions

- "What were sales last month?" → Q&A visual answers instantly
- "Why did metric X drop?" → Copilot root-cause analysis
- Complex, multi-table questions still require analyst expertise

## Becoming More Valuable

### Prompt Engineering for AI

- Knowing what questions to ask the AI
- Framing prompts that produce accurate, actionable outputs
- Iterating on AI responses to refine analysis

### Data Modeling

- AI works only as well as the underlying semantic model
- Designing clean star schemas, relationships, and naming conventions is the foundation
- This skill becomes more critical as AI is layered on top

### Business Context and Domain Knowledge

- AI identifies patterns; humans determine if they matter
- Understanding what insights actually drive decisions is irreplaceable
- Domain expertise cannot be learned by an AI trained on generic data

### Critical Thinking and AI Output Validation

- Always question AI attributions and correlations
- Design experiments to validate AI hypotheses
- Filter signal from noise in AI-generated insights

### Storytelling and Communication

- Turning AI outputs into business narratives
- Presenting AI findings in context stakeholders understand
- Translating data into decisions

### Strategy and Problem Framing

- Knowing which problems to solve with AI
- Prioritising analytical work by business impact
- Connecting AI insights to strategic decisions

## Comparison Table

| Criteria | Being Automated | Becoming More Valuable |
|----------|----------------|------------------------|
| Nature | Repetitive, query-based | Contextual, strategic |
| Output | Static reports | Decision recommendations |
| Skill type | Execution | Judgment |
| AI dependency | High | Low |
| Career trajectory | Declining | Rising |

## When to Use

Use this comparison to:
- Guide analyst upskilling roadmaps
- Scope AI feature adoption in a team
- Communicate to stakeholders what AI does and doesn't replace
