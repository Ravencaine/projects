Currently pursuing a degree in computer science, Mahnoor brings both a journalist's eye and a technical foundation to her coverage of how AI is reshaping the way we work and learn. Sign in to your XDA account No matter what your stance on AI was when OpenAI launched ChatGPT back in November 2022, it’s probably shifted at least a little since then. Even if you’re not consciously seeking it out, there’s a good chance you’re using AI in some form today. Whether it’s for surface-level tasks like summarizing emails, answering quick questions (essentially a Google Search replacement), or quietly powering the apps you already rely on, chances are you open an AI tool at least once or twice a day. I've tried plenty of AI setups over the last two years. One tool I've always found myself coming back to is NotebookLM . I've been using it since its early Google Lab days, and it's an incredibly important part of my workflow. Nowadays, Claude has become the other half of that equation. Together, they make up the most reliable AI workflow. Claude and NotebookLM are both fundamentally different tools Different by design, and that's the point I'm a huge fan of pairing tools together , and something I've learned over time is that tools complement each other when they're different rather than when they overlap. Now, Claude is fundamentally very similar to all the chatbots you've likely been tinkering with for a while, like ChatGPT and Gemini. You ask it a question, it gives you an answer. You give it a task, it gets to work. What makes Claude different, though, is how it interacts with you. Unlike ChatGPT and Gemini, I've found that Claude pushes you to think more. Rather than agreeing with you and glazing everything you do, Claude is more willing to push back and help you build on your ideas rather than just executing them. Anthropic, the maker of Claude, has also been going all-in on launching new features that differentiate Claude from the rest of the AI chatbot market. Claude Code , Cowork, Skills , and MCP integrations are just a few examples. NotebookLM, though, is a completely different tool. It's an AI research assistant, and rather than simply answering your questions from its training data or the web, it works exclusively with the sources you give it. You upload documents, articles, videos, links, images, and more, and NotebookLM grounds each response in that material. You get clickable citations alongside every answer NotebookLM gives you. The best part about NotebookLM is that it doesn't limit you to uploading only a single source. You can add a bunch of sources to your NotebookLM notebook, and it then synthesizes information across all of them. This helps you connect dots between sources that you might not have caught on your own, which is exactly what you need when you're working with a lot of material. Finally, NotebookLM also has different features that let you work with all your material in different ways. You can convert the sources within your notebook into Audio Overviews , Video Overviews, Mind Maps, Flashcards, and more. Ultimately, both of them are solving very different problems, which is exactly why they work so well together. Everything starts (and ends) in NotebookLM It’s where all the context lives Close No matter what type of project I'm working on, my process almost always begins the same way. I take whatever material I already have and throw it into a fresh NotebookLM notebook. Whether it's a coding project I want to begin or an exam I'm studying for, the type of project really doesn't matter. The goal is to make sure that I have a grounded starting point that I can keep updating and refer back to before I do anything else. It's like having a note-taking hub that keeps everything tied to real sources instead of scattered across different documents. For example, if I'm working on a coding project — say building a tool that integrates with the Spotify API — I'll upload the API documentation directly into NotebookLM. Instead of skimming through pages and pages of documentation, I can just ask NotebookLM the exact question I have and get a cited answer pulled straight from the source. When it comes to school, I have dedicated notebooks created for each course I'm taking, and they're where everything begins. Lecture slides, textbook chapters, past papers, my own notes — it all goes in. When I'm reviewing for an exam or working on an assignment, I'm not digging through folders trying to find where I read something. I just ask inside the notebook. Claude comes into the mix once the NotebookLM notebook is set up They work in harmony Close Once the NotebookLM notebook is all set up, Claude doesn't simply take it from there. Instead, it works alongside it. For example, there are plenty of moments while I'm interacting with NotebookLM where I hit a complete wall. Given that NotebookLM only works with the sources I've uploaded, if it's not mentioned in my sources, the tool is simply no good for it. So when I need context that goes beyond my sources, or I want to break down a concept further than what my uploaded material covers, I bring Claude in. I'll take what NotebookLM gave me, bring it over to Claude, and ask it to explain it differently or help me think through how to apply it.
