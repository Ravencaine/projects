## Summary

Beginner's personal narrative: author's journey from seeing a sales dataset as "just numbers" to using Power BI to surface business insights. Covers data preparation (check for missing values, fix incorrect data, remove unnecessary records), dashboard build, and one substantive analytical finding — cheap consumable products (batteries, cables, headphones) outsell expensive items (laptops) in quantity despite contributing less revenue.

## Key Claims

1. Data preparation is invisible in the final dashboard but is one of the most important steps — "the most attractive dashboard is useless if the data behind it is incorrect"
2. Every visualization should answer a business question, not just display data
3. High customer demand is not connected to product prices — cheap products bought frequently; expensive products bought infrequently
4. Understanding quantity-sold patterns helps businesses plan inventory
5. Business analytics is not about collecting data — it is about turning information into meaningful insights people can understand and use
6. A clean dataset always leads to insights

## Analytical Finding

| Product Type | Price | Purchase Frequency | Quantity Sold | Revenue |
|-------------|-------|-------------------|--------------|---------|
| Consumables/accessories (batteries, cables) | Low | High (replace often) | High | Low |
| Major appliances (laptops) | High | Low (planned, infrequent) | Low | High |

Neither metric alone tells the full story — use both quantity and revenue as separate measures.

## Notable Details

- Dataset: Sales_September_2019, 11,000+ records
- Three business questions the dashboard aimed to answer: which products sell most, which make money, how data helps decisions
- Author: first-time Power BI dashboard builder
