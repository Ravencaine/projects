They fail in the first three seconds.  
Someone opens the report, doesn’t find the number they’re looking for, exports it to Excel, and never opens it again. The model was fine. The measures were correct. Nobody used it.  
I put together 10 rules I keep coming back to — each one with a side-by-side example so you can see the difference, not just read about it:  
→ Design for the decision, not the data  
→ Put the answer in the top-left  
→ Bars beat pies (nearly always)  
→ Colour is a signal, not decoration  
→ Four KPIs, not fourteen  
→ Never show a naked number  
→ Speak business, not schema  
→ Filter with intent, not “just in case”  
→ Flat, aligned, and quiet  
→ Watch someone use it for five minutes  
The last one is the cheapest, and the one almost nobody does. Could you hand your report to a stakeholder, give them one task, then stay completely silent? Every hesitation you watch is a design note.  
The best dashboard isn’t the one with the most insight. It’s the one people open on Monday morning.
