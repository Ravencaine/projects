## 1\. Category-Wise Contribution

> *Example: Sales by Product Category Why Use: Shows how much each category contributes to total sales with a clear percentage visual.*

## 2\. Market Share Representation

> *Example: Market share of different brands or competitors Why Use: Highlights relative dominance in a circular layout, good for stakeholder presentations.*

## 3\. Budget vs Actual Breakdown

> *Example: Budget allocation vs actual spending by department Why Use: Easily communicates proportions within a total budget.*

## 4\. Customer Segmentation

> *Example: Distribution of customers by segment (e.g., Gold, Silver, Bronze) Why Use: Visualizes customer spread for marketing or service decisions.*

## 5\. Survey or Poll Results

> *Example: Percentage of user responses (e.g., Satisfied, Neutral, Unsatisfied) Why Use: Simple and visually appealing way to show proportions in sentiment analysis.*

## 6\. Regional Sales Distribution

> *Example: Sales contribution from different regions or zones Why Use: Good at displaying performance variation across multiple geographies.*

## 7\. Employee Role Distribution

> *Example: Percentage of employees in different departments or job roles Why Use: Helps HR teams understand resource distribution at a glance.*

## ⚠️ When Not to Use Donut Charts

- When you have more than 5–6 categories (too cluttered)
- When exact values matter more than proportions
- When you need to show trends over time (use bar/line charts instead)
