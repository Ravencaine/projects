# Camden Success Story: 56% Cost Reduction via Heat-Map Targeting

Dr. Jeffrey Brenner used block-by-block medical and crime data mapping in Camden, New Jersey to identify that 1% of patients drove 30% of costs — then cut those costs by 56% through targeted intervention.

## Definition

Brenner mapped hospital billing records from three main hospitals alongside crime statistics. He found two city blocks (a large nursing home and a low-income housing complex) where nine hundred residents accounted for four thousand hospital visits and $200 million in costs over 6.5 years. One patient had 324 admissions in five years; the most expensive cost $3.5 million.

## Key Points

- The intervention model: assign a nurse practitioner and social worker to the sickest, most expensive patients
- Provide preventative care, health monitoring, and substance abuse treatment to reduce emergency visits and hospitalization
- Early results: 56% cost reduction in the targeted population
- The methodology (geospatial cost heat map + high-utilizer identification) is now a standard population health analytics approach
- This is the "Fourth Factor of Production" applied to healthcare: letting data find the patients who need help most

## Examples

- Brenner worked against police objections to access the data
- The two highest-cost blocks had fundamentally different profiles: elderly nursing home residents vs. low-income housing tenants — requiring different intervention strategies
- The same methodology applies to any high-cost population: retail loss prevention, fleet maintenance, utility theft
