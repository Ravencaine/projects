# Customer Segmentation in Retail Analytics

Categorizing customers into groups (Consumer, Corporate, Home Office) to understand purchasing behavior and target marketing and retention strategies.

## Definition

Customer segmentation divides the customer base into distinct groups based on shared characteristics — typically using a Segment dimension in the data model. Each segment may exhibit different purchasing patterns, average order values, and product preferences.

## Key Points

- Common retail segments: Consumer (individual buyers), Corporate (business buyers), Home Office (small business/home use).
- Segment analysis reveals which groups drive the most revenue, profit, and order volume.
- Different segments may respond to different marketing approaches — personalization requires segment awareness.
- Segment performance can vary significantly by region and product category.
- Monitoring segment trends over time helps identify which groups are growing or declining.

## Examples

| Segment | Total Sales | Avg Order Value | Top Category |
|---------|-------------|-----------------|--------------|
| Consumer | $650,000 | $120 | Technology |
| Corporate | $380,000 | $450 | Office Supplies |
| Home Office | $120,000 | $200 | Furniture |

Corporate has fewer orders but higher AOV — different acquisition and retention strategies required.
