# Revenue vs Profit Distinction

Revenue is the total income generated from sales; profit is what remains after deducting all costs. Confusing the two leads to bad prioritization decisions in retail.

## Definition

**Revenue** (Total Sales) is the sum of all transaction amounts before any costs are subtracted. **Profit** is revenue minus the cost of goods sold (COGS), operational expenses, discounts, and any other deductions.

## Key Points

- Revenue answers "how much did we sell?" — profit answers "how much did we earn?"
- A product or category can have high revenue but low or negative profit if discounts are deep or COGS is high.
- Retail dashboards must display both metrics side-by-side; showing revenue alone overstates financial performance.
- Profit analysis reveals operational efficiency, not just top-line volume.
- Comparing revenue and profit by category exposes products that consume resources without generating proportionate returns.

## Examples

| Category | Revenue | Profit | Profit Margin |
|----------|---------|--------|---------------|
| Technology | $500,000 | $85,000 | 17% |
| Office Supplies | $300,000 | $15,000 | 5% |
| Furniture | $400,000 | -$20,000 | -5% |

Furniture shows strong revenue but negative profit — discount strategy or COGS requires review.
