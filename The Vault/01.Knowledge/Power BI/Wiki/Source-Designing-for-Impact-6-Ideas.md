# Designing for Impact: 6 Ideas to Enhance the UX and Accessibility of Power BI Dashboards (Isabelle Bittar)

> **Type:** article
> **Author:** Isabelle Bittar
> **Published:** 2024-03-02
> **URL:** https://medium.com/the-bi-corner/designing-for-impact-6-ideas-to-enhance-the-user-experience-and-accessibility-of-your-power-bi-a3096742d9eb
> **Routed to:** Power BI
> **KB routing note:** UX/accessibility principles, not DAX-specific — Power BI KB

## Summary

6 UX and accessibility design ideas illustrated through an EDI (Equity, Diversity and Inclusion) dashboard case study: (1) design for non-Power-BI users, (2) design for non-data-savvy users, (3) container over content (layout), (4) white space and progressive disclosure, (5) design trends in Power BI, (6) designing for vulnerability.

## 6 Design Ideas

1. **Design for End-Users That Don't Know Power BI:** navigation, content hierarchy, summary page, alerts, tutorials, toolbar centralization
2. **Design for End-Users That Aren't Data-Savvy:** simple visualizations, avoid complex charts, drop-down slicers over drill-downs
3. **Container Over Content:** nav menu, support tools, filter panel, KPIs, key visualizations
4. **Less is Less and More is More:** highlight main points, detail buttons for deeper exploration, white space
5. **Riding Design Trends:** light mode, responsive, bold typography, micro-interactions, expanding color palettes
6. **Designing for Vulnerability:** emotional/psychological/situational vulnerabilities, political sensitivities, information that causes harm

## Key Claims

- End-users ≠ sponsors, project managers, or BI developers — design for the person consuming the report
- Non-data-savvy users expect dashboards to behave like professional apps/websites
- Container/layout matters more than the actual data work
- "Less is more" fails when users want more info — solution: progressive disclosure with detail buttons
- Design trends from web/app world shape user expectations for dashboards
- Displaying certain information can create political tension; removing clear associations can protect the dashboard's adoption
