# Big Data Healthcare ROI: 12–17% Cost Reduction Potential

A 2013 McKinsey report — cited by Dunlop — estimates that implementing big data strategies in US healthcare could reduce expenses by 12–17%, saving $300–450 billion per year.

## Definition

The claim rests on three drivers: pharmaceutical companies aggregating years of research into medical databases, healthcare providers digitizing patient records, and a broader shift toward evidence-based medicine. Big data algorithms provide the best available evidence to support clinical and administrative decisions.

## Key Points

- Health spending currently exceeds 17% of US GDP
- The McKinsey estimate is based on aggregating clinical records, pharmaceutical research databases, and insurance data
- The "Medicalized Smartphone" trend (2015 article) predicts wearables and smartphone attachments will generate massive new data streams — blood pressure, glucose, heart rhythm — enabling virtual physician visits and continuous monitoring
- IDC Health Insights (December 2013) identifies four transformative technology pillars: mobile, big data analytics, social, and cloud
- Venture capitalist Vinod Khosla predicted technology would replace 80–90% of doctors' decision-making roles — though this remains controversial
- Watson (IBM) has been applied to cancer treatment plans, synthesizing 2 million pages of medical journals and 600,000 clinical records to produce ranked recommendations

## Examples

- Johns Hopkins "Smartphone Physical" project uses smartphone-based medical devices to collect clinical data: body weight, blood pressure, heart rate, blood oxygen, ECG, ultrasound
- Watson oncology: doctor inputs patient symptoms → ranked treatment recommendations with confidence scores
