# End-User vs Sponsor vs Developer — Design Audience Atomic

> **Type:** atomic
> **Routed to:** Power BI
> **Primary source:** Isabelle Bittar — 2024-03-02

## The Three Audiences

| Audience | Who They Are | Who They Want |
|----------|-------------|---------------|
| **End Users** | People consuming the report daily | Simple, actionable insights |
| **Sponsors** | Paying for the project | Results that justify investment |
| **Maintainers** | BI developers after go-live | Maintainable, well-documented reports |

## The Rule

> Design for **end users:** not sponsors, not project managers, not BI developers.

## Why Sponsors Are Wrong to Drive Design

Sponsors often have strong opinions about what the report should look like — but they are not the ones using it every day. Their priorities (show everything, impress leadership, match existing reports) often conflict with good UX.

## Why Maintainers Are Wrong to Drive Design

Maintainers optimize for easy code — but their preferences (native features, standard patterns, minimal customization) may deliver poor user experiences.

## The Correct Approach

1. **Identify end users** during requirements gathering
2. **Validate designs with end users** (not just sponsors)
3. **Test with real users** before go-live
4. **Explain UX decisions** to sponsors using end-user data
