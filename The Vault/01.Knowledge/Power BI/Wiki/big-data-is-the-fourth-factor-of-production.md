# Big Data Is the Fourth Factor of Production

The claim that big data represents the fourth factor of production — alongside land, labor, and capital — reframes data as a fundamental economic input, not merely a byproduct of operations.

## Definition

Traditional economics identifies three factors of production: land (natural resources), labor (human effort), and capital (manufactured assets). The argument made in a December 2012 *Financial Times* article — cited by Dunlop — is that data has become a fourth factor because organizations increasingly base decisions on data-derived insight rather than intuition or hierarchy.

## Key Points

- Executives are becoming "wedded to numerical insight" when making strategic decisions
- Big data enables both intuitive and analytical thinkers to excel — creative leaders find unexpected patterns, data teams validate and present them
- Self-service BI tools (Power BI, Excel) put this capability in the hands of front-line workers rather than just executives
- Data scientists validate accuracy and presentation, replacing the "information-gathering middle manager" role identified in a 2015 *Wall Street Journal* article
- Small and medium businesses can achieve "big data" results using tools they already know — Excel and Power BI — without the infrastructure overhead of Netflix or Facebook

## Examples

- General Motors used data analysis to decide to stop selling Chevrolets in Europe, completing an analysis in days that previously took weeks
- Planet Fitness analyzes treadmill usage by location within the gym to optimize equipment rotation
- Startups use unlimited data access to push decision-making to front-line workers, eliminating the traditional middle-management information layer
