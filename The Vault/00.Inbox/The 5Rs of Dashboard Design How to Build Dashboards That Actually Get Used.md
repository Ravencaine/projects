## Design smarter dashboards that inform, engage and drive action

> You can read the full article using this [friend link](https://medium.com/@poojapawar0309/01130ead23ba?sk=cc6905db94e7f4c93b9721164e30cddb).

Let me tell you a quick story.

A few months ago, a product manager I know built a dashboard for her leadership team. It had every metric imaginable — traffic, conversions, churn, NPS, lifetime value, engagement scores, the works.

But guess what?

Nobody used it.

Why? Because **it wasn’t designed for action**. It was a beautiful pile of metrics… but not a dashboard that helped people *decide* anything.

And that’s a common trap.

So how do you avoid it?

Here’s a framework I use (and now teach): **The 5Rs of Dashboard Design**.

It helps you build dashboards that aren’t just seen — but remembered, relied on, and acted upon.

Let’s walk through it.

## 1\. Relevant: Show What Matters, Hide the Rest

A dashboard isn’t a data dump. It’s a decision tool.

Relevance means aligning your dashboard with the user’s actual goals and context. A sales manager doesn’t care about bounce rate. A marketer probably doesn’t need to see backend uptime.

You want your dashboard to feel like a well-written headline: clear, purposeful, and laser-focused.

**Ask yourself:**

- Who is this dashboard for?
- What decisions do they make daily, weekly, monthly?
- What 3–5 metrics drive those decisions?

**Real-world example:** An executive dashboard should highlight overall performance and risk indicators — not 30 granular metrics that bury the story. Save those for drill-downs.

## 2\. Readable: If They Can’t Read It, They Won’t Use It

You’ve got 5–8 seconds to get their attention. If your dashboard takes a mental map to navigate, you’ve already lost.

Readable dashboards are *instantly scannable*. Think clean layouts, consistent fonts, meaningful labels, and intuitive chart choices.

**Tips to boost readability:**

- Left-to-right, top-to-bottom flow.
- Use color with purpose — not decoration.
- Avoid pie charts unless you’re slicing pizza.
- Replace technical jargon with everyday language.

**Metaphor:** Your dashboard should feel like the front page of a great newspaper — not the appendix of a research paper.

## 3\. Responsive: Designed for Where & How It’s Used

People don’t just view dashboards at their desks anymore. They check them on tablets during meetings or skim them from their phones between flights.

Your dashboard needs to adapt — not just in size, but in interactivity and real-time value.

**Make it responsive by:**

- Using responsive design tools or mobile-optimized views.
- Allowing users to filter, sort, or drill into the data.
- Setting refresh rates based on how often decisions are made.

**Scenario:** If your operations dashboard is used during daily standups, it better be updated *daily* — if not live.

## 4\. Reliable: Trust Is the Bedrock

A dashboard can be gorgeous and fast — but if the numbers are wrong, it’s dead on arrival.

Data accuracy, consistent refresh cycles, and clear definitions are non-negotiable. Your users should never wonder, “Wait, what does this number even mean?”

**How to build reliability:**

- Automate data refresh and error checks.
- Document KPI definitions right inside the dashboard.
- Use stable, validated data sources — not ad hoc exports.

Add a timestamp showing when the data was last updated. It’s a tiny detail that builds massive trust.

## 5\. Remarkable: Make It So Good, They Brag About It

Yes — dashboards can be remarkable.  
Not flashy. Not filled with animations.  
But remarkable because they solve real problems in ways that are elegant, intuitive, and delightful.

When a dashboard *just works* — when it anticipates the user’s need and delivers insights in seconds — it gets remembered.

**What makes a dashboard remarkable?**

- Smart defaults and filters.
- A clear visual hierarchy.
- Thoughtful micro-interactions (like tooltips, drill-downs, or quick exports).
- An experience that’s smooth, not clunky.

**Small touch, big impact:** Add a simple “Next steps” section or action recommendation. People love dashboards that *tell them what to do next*.

## So What’s the Point of All This?

You don’t need more charts.  
You need dashboards that help people think better, act faster, and feel confident.

If you design with the **5Rs — Relevant, Readable, Responsive, Reliable, and Remarkable** — you’ll stop building dashboards that are *looked at* and start creating ones that are *used*.

And that’s when your dashboards become a superpower — not a spreadsheet.
